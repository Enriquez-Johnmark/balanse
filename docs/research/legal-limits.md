## Key conclusions

1. **Nothing found kills Balanse.** The debt-collection conduct rules (SEC MC 18-2019, RA 11765, BSP Circular 1160) bind lenders and other regulated financial service providers, not ordinary trade creditors or the software they use. *(Interpretation, M.)*
2. **Balanse should still follow those rules as product rules.** Courts and the NPC judge collection conduct by the same standards through the Civil Code (abuse of rights), the Data Privacy Act, cyberlibel and unjust vexation. The regulators' lists are the clearest available standard. *(Recommendation.)*
3. **Exclude lending and financing businesses as customers.** If a lender, financing company, online lending app or informal lender uses Balanse, MC 18, RA 11765 and NPC Circular 20-01 apply directly, and Balanse could look like its collection service provider. *(Recommendation; MC 18 covers third-party collection providers [1][3].)*
4. **Balanse is a personal information processor (PIP) for each creditor.** The creditor is the controller. Kompleto's privacy work (DPA terms, sub-processor list, 72-hour breach chain, NPC registration threshold) carries over [I1][I2].
5. **Never contact anyone but the debtor**: no family, references, employer, group chats, CC or public posting. This is banned for lenders [1][15][16] and is the core of "debt shaming" cases under privacy and libel law [18][25].
6. **Do not compute interest or penalties.** Conventional interest needs a written stipulation (Art. 1956); legal interest (6%) runs from demand (Arts. 1169, 2209; MB Circular 799). A wrong computed figure is a "false representation" risk. Show only amounts the creditor typed [24][46].
7. **The biggest BIR grey zone is CAS, and it is real but avoidable.** RMO 9-2021 counts as a CAS component any software a non-large taxpayer "opted to use" that is "utilized for recording … Accounts Receivable" or that "generates … Accounts Receivable/Payable Register … Billing Statements and Other Supplementary Receipts/Invoices", and "collection" facilities are named in the definition. If Balanse became the creditor's receivables record, each creditor would have to register it with their RDO, with a Joint Sworn Statement signed by the creditor and Balanse (§V.6). Balanse must stay a follow-up list of amounts already recorded and invoiced elsewhere, and must never issue invoices, receipts, statements of account or "billing invoices" [30][31][44][I3]. *(Facts OD, H; the reading is an interpretation, M.)*
8. **A reminder must never be the first bill.** Under RMC 77-2024, service providers must issue a Billing Invoice "upon billing instead of a Billing Statement or Statement of Account". Issuing a statement of account at the sale without an invoice counts as failure to issue an invoice (₱1,000–₱50,000 fine and 2–4 years' imprisonment, Tax Code §264(a)). Balanse reminders should therefore only follow up an invoice the creditor has already issued [30]. *(OD, H.)*
9. **Linking only to the creditor's own GCash/Maya/bank QR or their own gateway account keeps Balanse outside the payment-system rules** on the definitions found. Holding funds, collecting for many merchants under one account, or processing payments on a creditor's behalf would bring it in [34][35]. *(Interpretation, M.)*
10. **No Philippine anti-spam statute for email was found**: the Cybercrime Act's spam clause was struck down in 2014 [26]. SMS is the regulated channel: registered sender IDs, no links, opt-out keywords, quiet hours [36][37][I1].

## 1. Debt-collection conduct rules

### Facts

**SEC Memorandum Circular No. 18, s. 2019** (primary text read [1], OD, H). Addressed "To: All Financing and Lending Companies"; dated 19 Aug 2019, signed 20 Aug 2019, published 23 Aug 2019, effective 15 days after publication. It applies "without prejudice to the application of the New Civil Code, Revised Penal Code, … Data Privacy Act, and other applicable laws". §1: "FCs, LCs, and TPSPs hired by them may resort to all reasonable and legally permissible means to collect amounts due them under the loan agreement", but must "observe good faith and reasonable conduct". The unfair collection practices in §1 are:
- (a) "The use or threat of use of violence or other criminal means to harm the physical person, reputation, or property of any person";
- (b) "The use of threats to take any action that cannot legally be taken";
- (c) "The use of obscenities, insults, or profane language the natural consequence of which is to abuse the borrower and/or which amount to a criminal act or offense under applicable laws";
- (d) "Disclosure or publication of the names and other personal information of borrowers who allegedly refuse to pay debts", except as §2 allows;
- (e) "Communicating or threatening to communicate to any person loan information, which is known, or which should be known, to be false, including the failure to communicate that the debt is being disputed", except as §2 allows;
- (f) "The use of any false representation or deceptive means to collect or attempt to collect any debt or to obtain information concerning a borrower";
- (g) "Making contact at unreasonable/inconvenient times or hours, which shall be defined as contact **before 6:00 A.M. or after 10:00 P.M.**, unless the account is **past due for more than fifteen (15) days**, or the borrower has given express consent that the said times are the only reasonable or convenient opportunities for contact". The consent must be "evidenced by written, electronic or recorded means";
- (h) "Notwithstanding the borrower's consent, contacting the persons in the borrower's contact list other than those who were named as guarantors or co-makers".

§2 allows disclosure of borrower data only with written or recorded consent, to credit bureaus and other lenders, on court or agency order, to the lender's own collection agents and counsel, and to insurers. §3: an outsourced collector "shall be regarded as agent", and responsibility stays with the lender. §4: collectors, in-house or outsourced, must "disclose his/her full name or true identity to the borrower". §5 penalties: lending companies ₱25,000 for a first offence and ₱50,000 for a second; financing companies ₱50,000 and ₱100,000; a third offence up to ₱1,000,000, a 60-day suspension or revocation [1] (OD, H). Contemporary summaries agree on scope and dates [2][3][4][5] (N/TP, SR, M).

**Still in force.** No amending or replacing circular was found. "SEC MC 32-2024" was not found: one search found SEC 2024 circulars listed only to No. 12 [7] (SR, L–M). The SEC kept enforcing MC 18 in 2025–26, e.g. a ₱1,000,000 penalty on Surity Cash Lending (decision of 16 Sep 2025) [7] (N, SR, M). Earlier enforcement cited MC 18 together with §4.4 of the RA 11765 IRR on threats and contact-list collection [7] (N, SR, M).

**RA 11765, Financial Products and Services Consumer Protection Act**, approved 6 May 2022 (primary text read [8], OD, H). §3(e): a financial service provider "refers to a person, natural or juridical, which provides financial products or services that are under the jurisdiction of financial regulators" (BSP, SEC, IC, CDA, per §3(d)). §3(c) lists "savings, deposits, credit, insurance, pre-need and … HMO products, securities, investments, payments, remittances and other similar products and services". §4: the Act "applies to all financial products or services offered or marketed by any financial service provider". §5 makes the CDA the implementing agency "only with respect to cooperative offering financial products or services, such as … savings and credit". **§8(d)** (Fair and Respectful Treatment of Clients): "Financial Service providers are prohibited from employing abusive collection or debt recovery practices against their financial consumers." §13: the provider is "solidarily liable with accredited third-party service providers for their acts or omissions in marketing and transacting, which may include, but not limited to, debt collection". The IRRs were not read (the E-Library's TLS chain fails) [9][10] (OD, SR, M).

**BSP Circular No. 1160** was issued **28 Nov 2022** (not 2023). It implements RA 11765 for BSP-supervised institutions (BSIs) by amending the bank and non-bank manuals [11][12] (OD/TP, SR, M). A law-firm summary, read in full, says BSIs "are also prohibited from employing abusive collection or debt recovery practices", that collection agencies, counsel and other agents "are indispensable parties in complaints involving unfair collection practices", and that BSIs are solidarily liable with agents for conduct "which may include debt collection" [13] (TP, M). **The circular's own list of banned collection acts was not found**: bsp.gov.ph blocks downloads. Contact-hour rules for BSP-supervised credit-card collection predate it (Circular 454, 2004), and secondary sources conflict on the numbers [14] (TP, SR, L).

### Interpretation

- **Ordinary trade creditors are outside all three.** MC 18 is addressed only to financing and lending companies and the TPSPs they hire [1]. RA 11765 covers only providers of financial products "under the jurisdiction of financial regulators" (§3(e)) [8]. A supplier, contractor, landlord or school extending payment terms is neither. No source addressed trade creditors directly (M–H).
- **Balanse as a tool is outside them too**, as long as its customers are not regulated lenders. MC 18 §3 treats a lender's outsourced collector as its agent, and RA 11765 §13 makes providers solidarily liable with third-party service providers for debt collection [1][8]. A reminder tool used by a lender could be argued to be such a provider (M).
- **Grey cases:** a credit or multi-purpose cooperative chasing loan arrears is a financial service provider under the CDA (RA 11765 §5) [8]. A cooperative selling goods on credit is unclear (not found). BNPL sellers, pawnshops, "5-6" lenders and in-house financing (e.g. real-estate in-house financing) are lenders in substance.

### Recommendation

Exclude lenders, financing companies, online lending apps, pawnshops, credit cooperatives' loan collection and informal money-lending in the terms of service and with a sign-up question. Adopt the MC 18 list as Balanse's conduct standard for everyone (see §8).

## 2. Data Privacy Act (RA 10173) and the NPC

### Facts

- **Roles.** A PIP is a person "to whom a personal information controller may outsource the processing of personal data" (RA 10173 §3(i)). Outsourcing to a PIP is not "data sharing", so the IRR §44 outsourcing contract terms apply, not the data-sharing circular [I1] (statute/IRR, M–H).
- **Lawful basis (statute, read in full [21], OD, H).** RA 10173 §12 allows processing of personal information when at least one condition exists, including "(b) … necessary and is related to the fulfillment of a contract with the data subject" and "(f) … necessary for the purposes of the legitimate interests pursued by the personal information controller …, except where such interests are overridden by fundamental rights and freedoms of the data subject". NPC treats processing "necessary for the establishment, exercise, or defense of legal claims" as a possible legitimate interest under §12(f) (Advisory Opinion 2025-003); in AO 2025-002 (asset searches on defaulting bank borrowers) the NPC analysed the bank's claim under §12(f) [19] (OD, SR, M). NPC Circular 2023-07 requires a documented three-part legitimate-interest assessment (purpose, necessity, balancing), compliance by 13 Apr 2024 [20] (TP, SR, M). Commentary also names contract fulfilment (§12(b)) as a ground for debt-collection processing [19] (TP, SR, L–M). No NPC opinion applying §12(b) to collection was found.
- **NPC Circular 20-01 (14 Sep 2020), amended by NPC Circular 2022-02**, covers personal data processed for loan-related transactions by lending and financing companies and "any natural or juridical person" acting as such, SEC-authorised or not [15][16] (OD, SR, M). It bars requiring access to phone, email or social-media contact lists [15][17] (OD/N, SR, M). A news report on the 2022-02 amendment, read in full, says: processing must not be "excessive, and disproportional", including "processing for collection of debt outside of the guarantors provided by the borrower". A character reference is only for verifying the borrower's identity and information and "shall not be automatically treated as a guarantor" (§4). A guarantor's separate consent is required (§5(A)). §5(B) "expressly prohibits a lending company, financing company, or other persons acting as such to contact persons in the borrower's contact list other than those who were declared as guarantors" [17] (N, fetched, M–H). The NPC's own PDFs remain blocked.
- **Public posting.** NPC Advisory Opinion 2023-001 treated a condominium's plan to post delinquent units and amounts as processing of personal information subject to §12 and to transparency, proportionality and legitimate purpose; **its conclusion was not retrieved** [18] (OD, SR, L–M). Kompleto's sector research recorded legal commentary that posting debtors' names in school group chats is a likely DPA violation [I5].
- **Registration (NPC Circular 2022-04).** Issued 5 Dec 2022, effective 11 Jan 2023. **It applies to processors as well as controllers**: "PICs or PIPs operating their DPS in the Philippines that employs at least 250 individuals or processes sensitive personal information of at least 1,000 individuals, among others, are required to register". "PICs and PIPs not covered by mandatory registration must submit a Sworn Declaration and Undertaking for Exemption". New systems register within 20 days of starting. Certificates last one year and are renewed within 30 days before expiry. Sanctions include processing bans and fines [45] (TP, fetched, M–H). The "likely to pose a risk" and automated-decision-making triggers appear only in excerpts [45] (SR, M); the circular's own text is still blocked. Breach (72 hours, IRR §38, Circular 16-03) and the founder as DPO are covered in [I1] §4 and [I2] §3.

### Interpretation

- **The creditor is the controller; Balanse is its PIP** for debtor data (name, contact, amount, due date, replies). Balanse is a controller only for its own users' account data. This mirrors Kompleto [I1] (M).
- **A creditor has a lawful basis to remind its own debtor**: contract (§12(b)) for the debtor's own contract, or legitimate interest (§12(f)) documented per Circular 2023-07 [19][20] (M). That basis does **not** extend to the debtor's relatives, references or employer: contacting them fails the necessity and balancing tests, and for lenders it is expressly banned [16] (M).
- **Debt amounts are not "sensitive personal information"** under §3(l) as summarised in [I1] (M). Balanse stays below the SPI-of-1,000 trigger only if it never collects TINs, government IDs or similar. Whether debt data counts as processing "likely to pose a risk" under 2022-04 was not found; treat it as open.
- **Schools:** debtors are parents but the subject is a minor. Address the parent or guardian only, never the student [I5] (interpretation, M).

### Recommendation

Reuse Kompleto's privacy package (DPA with the nine IRR §44 clauses, sub-processor list, 24-hour processor-to-controller breach notice, DPO designation, sworn declaration until registration) [I1][I2][I4]. Give each creditor a one-page legitimate-interest assessment template. Put a short notice line in every message naming the creditor as controller and linking a privacy notice (RA 10173 §16 right to be informed, [I2]). Collect only name, contact channels, amount, due date and the creditor's own reference.

## 3. Civil Code: demand, delay and interest

### Facts

- **Art. 1169** (Civil Code, read [46], OD, H): "Those obliged to deliver or to do something incur in delay from the time the obligee judicially or extrajudicially demands from them the fulfillment of their obligation." Demand is not needed "(1) When the obligation or the law expressly so declare", (2) when the time of delivery was "a controlling motive for the establishment of the contract", or "(3) When demand would be useless". The article prescribes no form of demand.
- **Proof of receipt decides it.** *Pineda v. Zuñiga Vda. de Vega*, G.R. No. 233774, 10 Apr 2019 (read [22], OD, H): the creditor's demand letter was in evidence, but there was "no satisfactory proof that the letter was received". So delay "was not triggered by an extrajudicial demand", and the Court ran interest only from the judicial demand (the filing of the complaint). Admitting a demand letter's existence is "at most an admission of the demand letter's existence and due execution", not of its receipt.
- **Electronic messages (RA 8792, read [47], OD, H).** §6: information "shall not be denied legal effect, validity or enforceability solely on the grounds that it is in the data message". §7: electronic documents have "the legal effect, validity or enforceability as any other document or legal writing", and are "the functional equivalent of a written document" for evidence. §22: unless agreed otherwise, receipt occurs when the message enters the addressee's designated information system or, if none was designated, any of the addressee's information systems; if it goes to a non-designated system, receipt occurs when the addressee retrieves it.
- **No Supreme Court ruling on email or SMS as extrajudicial demand was found.** Commentary says an electronic demand can count if it clearly demands performance and sending, receipt and identity can be proved [22] (TP, SR, L–M).
- **Interest** (Civil Code, read [46], OD, H). Art. 1956: "No interest shall be due unless it has been expressly stipulated in writing." Art. 2209: on delay in paying money, damages are "the interest agreed upon, and in the absence of stipulation, the legal interest, which is six per cent per annum". Art. 1229: a penalty "may also be reduced by the courts if it is iniquitous or unconscionable". For loans or forbearance of money, goods or credit, **legal interest is 6% a year from 1 Jul 2013** under BSP-MB Circular 799 (2013), applied prospectively, as adopted in *Nacar v. Gallery Frames* (G.R. No. 189871, 2013) and restated in *Odiamar v. Valencia* (G.R. No. 213582, 2018) [24] (OD court decision, SR, H). The circular itself was not read (bsp.gov.ph blocked).

### Interpretation

- Art. 1169 prescribes no form, and RA 8792 §§6–7 forbid denying an email legal effect just because it is electronic. So an email that clearly asks for payment of a stated amount can plausibly be an extrajudicial demand (M). It is not settled by any ruling found, and *Pineda* shows that the creditor must prove **receipt**, not just sending. Delivery events, a reply or a read acknowledgement are the practical proof; a provider's "sent" status alone may not be enough (M). A reminder worded as a soft nudge ("baka nakaligtaan lang po") may not read as a demand at all (L–M).
- If Balanse computes interest or penalties it must know whether a written stipulation exists, the agreed rate, the date of default (which depends on demand) and whether a penalty is unconscionable. Getting any of these wrong puts a false amount in a message, which is the MC 18 "false information" harm and an abuse-of-rights risk [1][28] (M). Computing running balances also moves Balanse towards generating accounting records (§5).

### Recommendation

- **Do not compute interest, penalties or late fees.** Show only amounts the creditor entered, labelled as from the creditor's records, with the creditor's own invoice or contract reference.
- Keep Balanse's audit trail (approved text, send time, provider message ID, delivery and reply events) exportable per debt, so a creditor can prove what was sent and when. Do not market reminders as legal demand letters.
- Owner decision for later: whether to offer a clearly labelled "formal demand" template, which would need lawyer-reviewed wording.

## 4. Harassment and defamation risk

### Facts

- **Cyberlibel**: RA 10175 §4(c)(4) punishes libel (RPC Art. 355) committed through a computer system, one degree higher than ordinary libel; sources conflict on the resulting range [25] (TP, SR, M). *Disini v. Secretary of Justice* (G.R. No. 203335, 18 Feb 2014) upheld online libel for the original author [26] (TP, SR, M).
- **Unjust vexation**: RPC Art. 287 second paragraph covers conduct that "unjustifiably annoys or vexes" another; a positive act is required; the fine is ₱1,000–₱40,000 and/or arresto menor (1–30 days) after RA 10951 (2017) [27] (TP, SR, M). No case on collection texts was found.
- **Civil Code Arts. 19, 21, 26**: Art. 19 is a "primordial limitation on all rights"; a right exercised against it that causes damage is a legal wrong (*Unicapital v. Consing*, G.R. No. 175277); abuse of rights needs a right, bad faith and intent to prejudice (*Navarro-Banaria v. Banaria*, G.R. No. 217806) [28] (TP/OD summaries, SR, M). Commentary: a valid debt does not by itself show good faith; courts look at how, how often and to whom contacts were made [28] (TP, SR, L–M).
- **RA 11313 Safe Spaces Act §12** (gender-based online sexual harassment) covers online threats and unwanted sexual, misogynistic, homophobic or sexist remarks; no source applies it to collection [29] (OD/TP, SR, M).
- **RA 12010 (AFASA, 20 Jul 2024)** punishes social-engineering schemes that obtain financial-account credentials by deception, including posing as an institution through electronic communications [42] (OD/TP, SR, M).

### Interpretation

- Libel needs publication to a third person, so one-to-one messages to the debtor carry much less libel risk than group chats, CCs, posts or messages to relatives (M). Repetition and tone, not the existence of the debt, drive unjust-vexation and Art. 19/21 exposure (M).
- No source gives a legal maximum number of reminders. Any frequency cap is a product choice, not a legal line (not found).
- Payment-request messages with links look like scams to recipients and telcos; Balanse must never resemble an AFASA-style scheme (M).

### Recommendation: wording Balanse templates and the AI must never produce

- Threats: arrest, jail ("ipapakulong", "makukulong"), police, NBI, barangay blotter, court case ("kakasuhan"), lawyer, "blacklist", "credit record", "final legal notice", unless the creditor types it themselves (and see the open question on legal-action wording).
- Labels: scammer, manloloko, magnanakaw, estafa, "walang hiya", any insult or profanity; anything sexual, gendered or about appearance.
- Shaming: "ipopost", "sasabihan namin ang pamilya/amo mo", mentions of relatives, employer or neighbours.
- Impersonation: posing as a lawyer, court, government office or collection agency.
- Scam signals: requests for OTPs, PINs or passwords; payment to an account other than the creditor's registered one; shortened links.

## 5. BIR: invoices, statements and CAS

### Facts

- **Invoices (RR 7-2024, full text read via a third-party copy [30], OD, H).** §3(A.4): RA 11976 "operates to establish the invoice as the primary evidence for both sales of goods and services"; official, collection or payment receipts are "supplementary document[s] showing proof of payment". §2: a **Supplementary Document** "is a written document, other than sales or commercial invoice, which serves as source of accounting entries in the books of accounts". It includes "official receipt, delivery receipt, order slip, … collection receipt, bill of lading, **billing statement, statement of account** and any other documents, by whatever name it is known or called, … as long as they are used in the ordinary course of business and being issued to customers or otherwise". They "are not valid proof to support the claim of input taxes". §6(B.15): supplementary documents must print "THIS DOCUMENT IS NOT VALID FOR CLAIM OF INPUT TAX." Every invoice and supplementary document needs a BIR Authority to Print or, if system-generated, a registered system (§7; §6(B.21)).
- **Statements of account (RR 11-2024 and RMC 77-2024, BIR digests read [31][30], OD, H).** RR 11-2024 (13 Jun 2024) lets taxpayers strike out "Billing Statement/Statement of Account/Statement of Charges", stamp "Billing Invoice" and use the stock "until fully consumed". RMC 77-2024 (11 Jul 2024): "Service providers who billed their customers shall now issue a Billing Invoice upon billing instead of a Billing Statement or Statement of Account." A statement of account "when issued upon sale of goods and services, … shall be tantamount to failure to issue or non-issuance of Invoice". The penalty is a fine of ₱1,000–₱50,000 and imprisonment of 2–4 years (Tax Code §264(a)). Receipts may still be issued "upon subsequent collection or receipt of payment".
- **CAS (RMO 9-2021, primary text re-read [44], OD, H).** §III.3: "Components of CAS (or CBA) – any system/application/software adopted to generate accounting records, reports and/or documents, which includes online transaction or application/software that serves as ordering, booking, **collection**, receipting/invoicing and other related business transaction facilities. This system … may or may not be integrated to or interfaced with CAS or CBA." §III.12 counts "billing statement, statement of account" as supplementary receipts "forming part of the books of accounts". §IV.2 brings in non-large taxpayers "who opted to use" components, including:
  - (ii) "Any system/software that is utilized for recording Sales, Purchases, **Accounts Receivable**, Accounts Payable, Inventory, and Payroll";
  - (iii) "Any system/software which generates Subsidiary Ledgers (such as … **Accounts Receivable/Payable Register**, etc.) and Other Accounting Records (such as … **Billing Statements** and Other Supplementary Receipts/Invoices)";
  - (iv) "generates reports as required by the BIR".
- **What registration would mean.** The obligation falls on the taxpayer-user: register the "System" with its RDO before use and comply with Annex B's functional and technical requirements (§V.1). Where a third-party software provider is used, the system must be registered "prior to the effectivity of their contract", with a **Joint Sworn Statement executed by both parties** declaring the software "has no facility that may suppress sales/income" (§V.6). Major enhancements need re-registration (§V.8). The BIR issues an Acknowledgement Certificate within 3 working days; no pre-evaluation or demo is required (§V.3, §V.5) [44].
- **No replacement found.** No issuance superseding RMO 9-2021 was found [33] (SR, M). RR 11-2025 (27 Feb 2025, BIR digest read [48], OD, H) requires e-invoices from, among others, taxpayers in "electronic commerce … or internet transactions" and those using CAS or "other invoicing software". It exempts micro taxpayers and gives covered taxpayers one year from effectivity. This concerns how Balanse's **owner** invoices their own customers ([I2] summary table), not Balanse's reminders. RR 26-2025 was found only as a scanned PDF and was not read. RR 9-2009 set the original electronic-records rules that RMO 9-2021 refers to [32] (OD summary, SR, M).
- **Kompleto's ADR 0004** keeps Kompleto from keeping books, computing tax, producing BIR-format files or archiving documents [I3].

### Interpretation

- **A reminder that follows an issued invoice is not an invoice and not a supplementary document.** It does not evidence a sale and is not a "source of accounting entries in the books of accounts" (RR 7-2024 §2); the creditor's invoice already is (M–H).
- **A reminder that replaces the bill is a problem for the creditor.** If a service provider never issued an invoice and Balanse's message is the first statement of what is owed, it works as a billing statement "upon billing". RMC 77-2024 treats that as non-issuance of an invoice, a criminal offence under §264(a). The creditor carries the liability, but Balanse would be the tool used (M).
- **Risk rises if Balanse produces a document.** A PDF titled "Statement of Account" is a supplementary document named in RR 7-2024 and RMO 9-2021 §IV.2.c.iii. Anything titled "Invoice" or "Billing Invoice" needs an ATP or a registered system. Either would make Balanse a CAS component (H on the text, M on the application).
- **CAS: a precise reading.** Three of the RMO's hooks touch Balanse: "collection" facilities (§III.3), software "utilized for recording … Accounts Receivable" (§IV.2.c.ii) and software that "generates … Accounts Receivable/Payable Register" (§IV.2.c.iii). All three are tied to the purpose in §III.3, software "adopted to generate accounting records, reports and/or documents", and to a taxpayer who "opted to use" it as part of its books (M). The line that keeps Balanse outside: the creditor's books of accounts and receivables records live elsewhere (manual books or their accounting software); Balanse holds a working copy of amounts already invoiced, for follow-up only; and it generates no accounting record, register, statement or BIR report (M). **No BIR ruling or issuance applying RMO 9-2021 to a reminder tool was found**, so the reading is unconfirmed.
- **What crossing the line costs** (inference from §V, M–H). The outcome is not a ban but paperwork: each creditor would register Balanse with their RDO, Balanse would co-sign a Joint Sworn Statement for every creditor, meet Annex B's technical requirements and re-register major changes. Under RR 11-2025, a registered system that generates invoices would also need structured e-invoice data [48]. For a ₱0-budget, self-serve product this is a commercial kill-risk, so the design must stay clear of it.

### Recommendation (an ADR 0004 equivalent for Balanse)

- Balanse never issues, numbers or titles anything as an invoice, billing invoice, official receipt, collection receipt or statement of account.
- Each debt carries the creditor's own invoice reference and date, and reminders refer to it ("Invoice #123 dated 5 Oct"). A debt can be entered without one only after the creditor confirms an invoice was issued. Balanse explains why: its messages follow up a bill and never replace it (RMC 77-2024).
- "Marked as paid" is a status, not a receipt.
- No receivables register, aging report or export styled as a ledger or for BIR filing; no running balances that post sales or payments.
- The terms of service say Balanse is not a book of accounts, an invoicing system or a receivables register, and that the creditor's records live elsewhere.
- A future "attach statement" or invoicing feature needs a new owner decision and a fresh check.
- Open question: a written BIR ruling request confirming a reminder tool is not a CAS component (cost and timing not found).

## 6. Payments

### Facts

- **RA 11127** (National Payment Systems Act, approved 30 Oct 2018; read [49], OD, H). §4(l): an operator is "any person who provides clearing or settlement services in a payment system, or defines, prescribes, designs, controls or maintains the operational framework for the system". §4(p): a payment system is "the set of payment instruments, processes, procedures and participants that ensures the circulation of money or movement of funds". §4(r): a service provider is "an entity that provides process arrangements, technology or infrastructure to operators of a payment system". §10: operators "shall register with the Bangko Sentral".
- **BSP Circular 1049** (published 16 Sep 2019, effective 1 Oct 2019, per a summary read in full [34], TP, M–H; primary PDF blocked). An OPS is anyone who "(1) maintains a platform that enables payments or fund transfers …; (2) operates the system or network that enables payments or fund transfers to be made through the use of a payment instrument; (3) provides a system that processes payments on behalf of any person or the government; and (4) other activities similar … as may be determined by the Monetary Board". New OPS register within one month of starting operations.
- **Examples (corrected).** A law-firm column says OPS "may include online merchants or billers, payment gateways, bill payment aggregators", and that foreign providers serving PH customers must register too [34] (TP, fetched, M). The first pass attributed a longer list of examples to Philstar; the Philstar article, read in full, gives none.
- The BSP ordered an unregistered operator (Lyka) to halt in 2021 [34] (OD, SR, M). For bills payment, the operator of the service registers, not its agent banks (BSP M-2020-004) [35] (N, SR, M).
- No general merchant or "technical service provider" exemption was found [34] (not found). E-money issuer rules (BSP Circular 649) were **not checked** in this pass.

### Interpretation (M)

- **Out of scope:** showing the creditor's own static GCash/Maya/InstaPay QR, bank details or a link to the creditor's own PayMongo/Xendit payment link, with money going straight to the creditor and Balanse never seeing it. Balanse then maintains no payment platform, processes nothing on anyone's behalf, and provides no technology to a payment-system operator (RA 11127 §4(r)). The column's "online merchants or billers" remark concerns how a creditor collects, not a reminder tool, and is not BSP text.
- **In scope or close:** collecting into Balanse's own account and remitting (payment facilitator / bills payment); one merchant account with sub-merchants or platform payouts (platform provider); stored balances or credits usable for payments (possible e-money); a "Pay now" checkout run through Balanse's merchant account. Creating payment links on a creditor's own gateway account through its API key is untested: no exemption text found.

### Recommendation

First version: static payment details and creditor-owned links only. Payment details shown in messages may change only after the creditor re-authenticates, and every message carries a line such as "Magbabayad lang po sa [registered account]; hindi po namin hihingin ang inyong OTP o PIN" [42]. Any API payment integration needs a new decision and a fresh BSP check.

## 7. Messaging channels

### Facts

- **SMS (NTC broadcast rules).** MC 03-03-2005 (broadcast messaging) required opt-in for commercial broadcasts and barred broadcast/push messages **between 9:00 p.m. and 7:00 a.m.** MC 07-08-2018 requires double opt-in and free opt-out by "STOP", "END", "CANCEL", "UNSUBSCRIBE" and "QUIT" in any case [36] (OD, SR, M; E-Library unreachable). MC 04-07-2009, as quoted in contemporary news read in full: "Content and/or information providers shall not be allowed to send and/or initiate push messages as defined under MC No. 03-03-2005". The ban was aimed at "text spam that are mostly promotional advertisements and other forms of offerings" [36] (N, fetched, M). **Whether these rules reach one-to-one transactional messages was not found**; the texts address content and information providers and broadcast/push services.
- **Links in SMS.** On 12 Sep 2022 the NTC wrote to Smart, Globe and DITO to block "URLs, TinyURLS, SmartLinks and QR codes from malicious sites identified by a government database" [50] (N, fetched, M–H). The later September 2023 memo blocking all clickable links rests on excerpts [I1] §5 (SR, M). Semaphore rejects sender names not tied to a business [I1] §5.
- **Sender IDs and content (Twilio rules, read in full [37], VD, M–H; vendor claim).** "Sender ID Registration is required in the Philippines. Starting on April 07, 2025, messages with unregistered Sender IDs will be blocked." "Shortened URLs in message content are strictly not allowed"; a URL "can only be delivered if the URL is registered and the message is sent using a registered Alphanumeric Sender ID"; "Phone numbers in message content is also not allowed". "Money/loan" content is listed as prohibited. Other vendors add that IDs must match the business name (≤ 11 characters), need a letter of authorisation and may be overwritten [37] (VD, SR, M). NTC primary text on sender IDs: not found.
- **RA 11934 SIM Registration Act** (IRR NTC MC 001-12-2022) requires every SIM, including eSIMs, to be registered before activation [38] (OD, SR, M–H). Nothing on A2P SMS was found.
- **Email.** The Cybercrime Act's ban on unsolicited commercial communications (§4(c)(3)) was struck down in *Disini* (2014) [26] (TP, SR, H). **No other PH anti-spam statute for email was found.** RA 8792, read in full, governs the legal effect of electronic messages and has no anti-spam provision [47] (OD, H).
- **Messenger.** Businesses may reply within a 24-hour window after a person messages them; outside it, message tags allow only approved non-promotional updates, and misuse can restrict the Page [40] (VD, SR, M). Whether payment reminders fit a current tag: not found.
- **Viber.** The share scheme `viber://forward?text=` opens Viber with pre-filled text that "the user will either be able to edit … or simply send" [41] (VD, SR, M). Rakuten Viber terms on business use of personal accounts: not found.

### Interpretation

- The NTC broadcast rules target content providers and promotional push messages. A one-to-one transactional reminder sent through an aggregator is probably not a "broadcast/push message", but no text says so (L–M). Following their stricter hours and opt-out keywords costs little.
- Aggregators may flag payment-related SMS under "money/loan" content filters. Wording should read as a reminder from a named business, never as a loan or credit offer (M; vendor rule).
- Copy-and-open on Viber or Messenger means the creditor sends from their own account; Balanse sends nothing on those platforms (M).
- Absence of an email spam law does not remove DPA duties on email addresses (§2) (M).
